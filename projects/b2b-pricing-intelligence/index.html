Executive overview

This dashboard screens historical B2B transaction patterns to identify customer-SKU pricing relationships that warrant commercial review. A flagged case is not an automatic recommendation to increase price.

How to read these cases

Pricing Review: materially below the SKU customer median with quantity at or below the median; review whether commercial terms justify the gap.
Possible Volume Justification: materially below median with above-median volume; a discount may be commercially justified.
Maintain / No Material Gap: no material below-benchmark gap under the screening rule.
Insufficient Evidence: fewer than 10 SKU customers, 30 SKU orders or 3 customer-SKU orders.

Effective price weights prices by units sold. Latest price is the latest transaction price, which may reflect a different order quantity. Benchmarks cover all identified customers for each SKU and do not change with the segment filter. A common SKU and quantity alone do not establish fully comparable contract or service terms.

Model and limitations

Monthly log(quantity) = intercept + beta × log(effective price). At least 12 observed months, 4 distinct prices and 5% price and quantity variation are required. Displayed sensitivity models also require -3 ≤ beta < 0, p < 0.05 and R² ≥ 0.20.

Seasonality, inventory availability and customer mix are uncontrolled; promotions and contracts are unavailable. Association does not prove price caused demand changes. These exploratory filters do not validate a causal demand model.

Commercial Pricing Simulator — Scenario Analysis

Inputs in this simulator are user-defined scenarios and are not available in the UCI dataset.

The historical analysis evaluates what customers paid and how purchasing behaviour varied with price. The Commercial Pricing Simulator evaluates whether a proposed price is commercially sustainable after considering cost-to-serve.

Enter an explicit zero for costs or claim probability you choose to exclude. Claim probability is a per-unit assumption across the whole stated warranty period; this simple model assumes at most one claim per unit. Changing the period does not automatically change probability or cost, and requires reconfirmation. Costs use a consistent per-unit basis and currency; tax is not modelled.

Calculation definitions

Warranty cost = Claim probability × Average claim cost.
Cost to serve = Product cost + Expected warranty cost + Additional service cost.
Required price = Cost to serve / (1 − Target gross margin).
Gross profit per unit = Selling price − Cost to serve.
Gross margin = Gross profit / Selling price.

Margin here uses the scenario's fully loaded cost-to-serve; accounting treatment of service and warranty costs may differ. Required margin in the waterfall is the currency amount needed above cost to serve, not a markup percentage.

Historical Customer Behaviour + Market / Customer Benchmark + Cost to Serve + Warranty Exposure + Target Margin → B2B Pricing Decision

Warranty exposure is already included in cost to serve and must not be added twice. The framework connects the considerations conceptually; no simulator inputs are merged into historical results.

Methodology & limitations

Customer profile → Price dispersion → Volume comparison → Commercial review. Exploratory historical price response and user-defined commercial scenarios answer separate questions.

Descriptive analysis and diagnostic screening

Effective price = revenue / quantity. Price gap = (SKU median customer effective price - customer effective price) / SKU median customer effective price. Customer quantity is compared with the median total quantity of customers buying the same SKU. The configurable gap threshold defaults to 15%; evidence requires 10 SKU customers, 30 SKU orders and 3 customer-SKU orders. None of these thresholds proves an economically optimal price.

Revenue percentile segments: Small below 50%, Medium 50-80%, Large at least 80%, Strategic at least 95% with 5 orders. These are descriptive groups, not contractual tiers. Accounts are a B2B proxy; business status is not verified.

Public transactional dataset with no product cost, customer contracts, rebates, competitor prices, warranty terms, service costs or negotiated pricing reasons. Historical relationships are observational rather than causal. Review these missing commercial inputs before pricing decisions.

The executive revenue KPI covers identified customers only. Missing-customer sales are excluded from customer analysis. Positive extremes, exact duplicates and non-product stock codes are retained under the original cleaning rules; inspect notebook data-quality outputs when reviewing unusual cases.
