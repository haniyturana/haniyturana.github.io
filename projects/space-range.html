Independent Retail Analytics Case Study

Retail Space & Range Optimisation

Translating sales, gross profit, shelf productivity, stock cover and store characteristics into practical assortment decisions.

Urban premium · Balanced strategy

Independent portfolio work This independent portfolio case study uses synthetic data. It is not based on Watsons data and does not represent Watsons’ actual operations, results or commercial decisions.

Business problem

Historical shelf allocation can drift away from current commercial reality.

Legacy space decisions may no longer reflect customer demand, gross-profit contribution, sales productivity, stock turnover, store mission, SKU duplication, range relevance or availability constraints.

The result can be low sales per shelf metre, excess inventory, duplicated choices, weak category profitability, stock-outs in productive ranges and inconsistent assortments across store types.

Decision framework

From contribution metrics to an implementable decision

Sales contribution→ Gross profit→ Space share→ Shelf productivity→ Stock cover→ Availability→ Duplication→ Store mission→ Commercial checks→ Expand · Retain · Reduce · Review

A product should not automatically receive more shelf space simply because its sales share exceeds its space share. Gross profit, stock turn, category role, availability, substitution risk, store mission and commercial agreements must also be considered.

Recommended actions

RetainBalanced contribution and strategically important core assortment.
ReduceAcceptable demand but weak productivity relative to space occupied.
Review / delistLow sales, low margin, duplication and excessive stock cover.

How I approached it

Business question → metrics → trade-offs → recommendation → validation

1. DiagnoseCompare sales, profit, space, stock cover, availability and duplication.
2. SegmentDefine different needs for urban, family and value-focused stores.
3. ScoreCalculate product and space productivity using consistent measures.
4. SimulateReallocate space and estimate conservative commercial impact.
5. RecommendAssign Expand, Retain, Reduce or Review actions.
6. ValidateCheck availability, commitments, substitution, cost and operational feasibility.

Risks and limitations

Recommendations require commercial validation.

Synthetic dataNo customer-path or heat-map dataNo supplier-funding informationNo exact planogram dimensionsNo product-substitution modelNo implementation-cost data

Proposed validation approach

  1. Select comparable test and control stores.
  2. Monitor sales, gross profit, availability, stock turn and customer response.
  3. Evaluate results over an agreed period.
  4. Adjust the recommendation before broader rollout.

Projected outcomes require controlled store testing and post-implementation measurement.
